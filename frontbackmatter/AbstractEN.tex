	\chapter*{Abstract}
The automation of the Adjacent Controller position in air traffic control offers significant potential to increase the efficiency of air traffic controller training within the simulation environments of \ac{DFS} Deutsche Flugsicherung GmbH. To achieve this, the present thesis develops an approach based on Reinforcement Learning (\ac{RL}). However, a major drawback of existing \ac{RL} methods in this domain is an unsteady control behavior known as \enquote{jittering}. This phenomenon manifests in unnecessary, minor course corrections that significantly reduce both the realism and the acceptance of automated controllers.
\medskip

To address this issue, a hybrid action space is designed. It combines the discrete decision of whether to intervene at all (\emph{No-Operation}) with continuous control variables for executing course changes. Technically, this is implemented through a targeted modification of the Proximal Policy Optimization (\ac{PPO}) algorithm: By utilizing \enquote{Gradient\hyp{}Gating}, the agent is able to stably learn the conditional dependencies inherent in this mixed action space.
\medskip

The performance and technical feasibility of the developed architecture were evaluated across multiple test series, encompassing both a standard environment and a simplified air traffic simulation. The results indicate that while the approach does not significantly increase the final reward, it achieves faster convergence and a substantially improved utilization of non-interventions (\emph{Noops}). The resulting reduction in intervention frequency confirms the intended calmer and more efficient control behavior. Consequently, this work provides a successful proof of concept for employing mixed action spaces to prevent redundant control commands in air traffic management.
